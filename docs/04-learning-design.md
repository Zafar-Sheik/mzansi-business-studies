# Learning design & study tools

Business Studies has a large volume of content, and most learners study it by rereading notes – the least effective method. BusinessMzansi is built on methods that work.

## Inside every lesson

1. **Hook** – a familiar South African business situation.
2. **Small steps** – one idea per step, 2–12 minutes each, with a visible progress bar.
3. **Do something** – every step has an activity: sort scenarios, tap a model, put a procedure in order, match pairs, type an answer.
4. **Memory hooks** – a mnemonic for every list that has to be learnt (pink callouts).
5. **Exam it** – an exam-style question with the marking guideline at the end of each lesson.

## The evidence behind it

- **Retrieval practice.** Quizzes, flashcards, drills and "write your answer before you open the memo" all make learners recall, which builds memory far better than rereading.
- **Spaced repetition.** Flashcards come back at growing intervals.
- **Worked examples with self-explanation.** Calculations are revealed one step at a time, each with a *why*.
- **Interleaving.** The Quiz Arena, drills and mock papers mix topics, the way the papers do.
- **Immediate, specific feedback.** Wrong answers are "not yet", with a hint about what went wrong.
- **Dual coding.** Models, tables and labs sit next to the words.

## Subject-specific design

- **Scenarios first.** Section B is scenario-based, so 172 short scenarios train the "identify, then quote" routine.
- **Marking-guideline language.** Memo facts are written the way NSC guidelines phrase them, so learners absorb the expected wording.
- **Two marks a fact.** Self-marking uses the real rule: a full fact earns two marks, capped at the question's total.
- **Essays demystified.** The essay planner shows sub-maximums, the cap of 32 for content and the eight LASO marks.

## Study tools

Weak-spot coach (what to work on next), focus sprints, progress dashboard, badges per topic, and printable worksheets and mock papers.
